# Scientific critical thinking

Evaluate the chain from question to evidence to conclusion. Critique the work,
not the researcher, and calibrate every finding to the available information.

## Review dimensions

1. **Claim** — Is it precise, falsifiable, and no stronger than the evidence?
2. **Evidence source** — Is it primary, current, representative, independently
   verified, and available at the claimed level of detail?
3. **Design** — Are comparison groups, controls, randomization, blinding,
   inclusion rules, temporal order, and measurement validity appropriate?
4. **Bias and confounding** — Consider selection, survivorship, measurement,
   publication, reporting, batch, site, temporal, and algorithmic bias.
5. **Statistics** — Check sample unit, independence, missing data, model
   assumptions, multiplicity, effect sizes, uncertainty, power, overfitting, and
   researcher degrees of freedom.
6. **Logic** — Look for circular reasoning, post hoc explanations, base-rate
   neglect, false dichotomies, inappropriate extrapolation, and correlation
   presented as causation.
7. **Robustness** — Ask whether the conclusion survives plausible preprocessing,
   model, cohort, threshold, seed, and measurement alternatives.
8. **Generalization** — Identify population, setting, time, domain, and scale
   boundaries.

## Output

For each material issue provide the exact claim or artifact, observed evidence,
why it matters, severity, and a concrete test or revision. Separate fatal flaws,
repairable weaknesses, and uncertainty caused by missing information. List
credible alternative explanations and evidence that would distinguish them.

Absence of detected problems is not proof of correctness. State which checks
were possible and which were not.
