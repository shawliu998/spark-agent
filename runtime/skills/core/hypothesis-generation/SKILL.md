# Hypothesis generation

Generate competing, falsifiable explanations rather than a single preferred
story.

## Inputs

Separate:

- observed facts and their uncertainty;
- literature-supported constraints;
- assumptions and measurement limits;
- unexplained pattern or research gap;
- resources, ethics, time, and compute constraints.

If the observation is not real or traceable, stop and request evidence rather
than building a mechanism on invented data.

## Process

1. State the phenomenon in measurable terms and identify plausible causal level:
   physical, molecular, cellular, organismal, behavioral, system, or algorithmic.
2. Generate several mechanistically distinct hypotheses, including a null,
   artifact/confounding explanation, and a simple baseline.
3. For each hypothesis specify the proposed mechanism, assumptions, supporting
   evidence, conflicting evidence, and at least one risky prediction.
4. Design observations or interventions that discriminate between hypotheses,
   not merely confirm each one separately. Include positive/negative controls,
   randomization or blocking, blinding where relevant, and expected failure
   modes.
5. Define measurable outcomes, uncertainty, analysis plan, and what result would
   reject or substantially weaken the hypothesis.
6. Rank by explanatory value, falsifiability, novelty, feasibility, ethical
   acceptability, and information gain. Select a primary and fallback direction.

## Output

Use a comparison table with columns for hypothesis, mechanism, predictions,
falsifier, controls, required evidence, feasibility, and main risk. End with the
smallest decisive next experiment and a clear statement that the hypotheses are
proposals, not findings.
